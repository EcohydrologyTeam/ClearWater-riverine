# Newly-wet cell artifacts: root cause, reconciliation, and punch list

Status date: 2026-05-30. Supersedes the open items in `SESSION_HANDOFF_2026-05-30_newly-wet-temperature.md`, which is now partly stale (see "Status corrections" below). ## What already exists (and is generic)

`reconstruct_newly_wet()` in `src/clearwater_riverine/transport.py` (def at line 21) already initializes each newly-wet cell to an inflow-weighted average of its donor neighbors' concentrations, which is the physically-correct "inherit the concentration of the water arriving from the adjacent cell" behavior. The arriving water carries the donor cell's concentration at time `t`, consistent with the first-order upwind advection used by the transport step (comment at transport.py:100-102). Ghost-cell boundary values supply the donor concentration when a newly-wet cell is fed only by an inflow boundary (transport.py:104-106).

Key properties:

- It is applied in the per-constituent loop (`for constituent_name, constituent in constituents.items():`, transport.py:646) and called once per constituent at transport.py:824. It is therefore generic across temperature, DOX, floating algae (chlorophyll-a), alkalinity, DIC, and the N and P species, not temperature-specific.
- It is on by default (`reconstruct_newly_wet=True` in both `ClearwaterRiverine.__init__`, model.py:148, and `TransportEngine.__init__`, transport.py:558).
- The gather cascade tries signed inflow at `t`, then signed inflow at `t+1` (staggered-time fallback), then an equal-weight average over any wet or ghost neighbor, accepting the first strictly-positive average (transport.py:175-191). Two relaxation passes let a wetting front advance one neighbor ring per iteration.

The critical limitation is that it only lifts, never lowers (transport.py:192-199): a candidate replaces the solver value only when the existing value is non-finite or the candidate is strictly larger. This is why it cures the cold/`c approximately 0` collapse but cannot suppress an over-hot value.

## The two artifact families and their distinct mechanisms

Both artifacts live in cells whose volume `V` passes through approximately zero at the wetting/drying front, but they arise in two different parts of the calculation.

Cold artifact, transport/discretization side, this repo. With `V_old = 0` the cell has no storage term to anchor the implicit solve, so the new concentration is determined almost entirely by face fluxes, roughly `c_new approximately sum(Q_in * c_nbr) / sum(Q_out)`. When the contributing neighbors are themselves newly-wet, dry, or NaN, the division degenerates and collapses the cell toward zero or a spuriously cold value. No physical mechanism produces freezing water at a September flood front, so this is a pure numerical artifact of the discretization at `V -> 0`. `reconstruct_newly_wet` patches it post-solve by substituting the donor-weighted inflow average.

Hot artifact, reaction/kinetics side, modules TSM. The heat-flux update is `dT = q_net * SA * dt / (V * rho * c_p)`. The plan-area surface `SA` stays finite while `V -> 0`, so `dT` scales as `1/V` and blows up. This one is mixed rather than purely numerical: a genuinely stable thin film can warm fast, but only when its residence time greatly exceeds the timestep. An advancing front is flushed within a step and cannot accumulate that surface heating, yet the kernel applies the full flux regardless. Rec B patches it by zeroing the kinetics delta below a depth threshold (a proxy for "advancing front, do not heat a film that is about to be flushed").

## Root cause: wetting/drying state and volume do not reconcile with HEC-RAS

Both artifacts trace to the same origin: cells that `ClearWater-riverine` treats as "wet with tiny `V`" but that HEC-RAS would either treat as dry (below its own wet/dry tolerance) or assign a different volume to via its sub-grid elevation-volume relationship. The patches address the symptoms on each side; the root-cause fix is to make `ClearWater-riverine`'s wet/dry decision and cell volume match what HEC-RAS used to produce the hydraulics.

Three specific mismatch points to investigate:

1. Wet/dry threshold mismatch. HEC-RAS applies a wetting/drying tolerance below which a cell is excluded from computation. `ClearWater-riverine` approximates this with `wet_dry_volume_threshold` (default `1.0e-3` m^3; `TransportEngine.__init__` transport.py:559, applied in `linalg.py` around lines 278-282) and an optional WET_MASK path (`wet_dry_metric` plus `wet_dry_threshold = {h_min: 0.01, V_min: 0.1}`, model.py:144-145). If these thresholds do not match HEC-RAS's, `ClearWater-riverine` processes cells RAS considered dry, and those tiny-`V` cells are exactly where both the `1/V` heating spike and the degenerate-diagonal collapse occur.

2. Volume and flux timing on the staggered grid. HEC-RAS stores face flows and cell volumes at offset half-steps. If `V` and `Q` are paired from slightly mismatched stamps at the front, the implied storage change is wrong precisely where `V` changes fastest. The donor-inheritance comment (transport.py:100-106) already encodes one staggered-time assumption; the volume side needs the same scrutiny.

3. Sub-grid bathymetry. A RAS cell volume is a nonlinear function of water-surface elevation through the cell's elevation-volume curve, so a cell can be "wet" (WSE above minimum ground) with very small `V`. Consuming RAS `V` directly inherits these tiny-`V` cells, and the discretization then misbehaves there. Reproducing the wet/dry decision RAS made (ideally reading any wet/dry indicator RAS writes to the HDF, rather than re-deriving it) would remove the offending cells at the source.

## The reconciliation: one residence-time criterion, applied symmetrically

The lift-only transport patch and the depth-threshold kinetics patch use two different proxies for the same underlying condition: "this cell is too thin or too short-lived to trust its raw solve." The physically-based criterion is residence time, not depth or volume: `tau = V / sum(|Q_out|)`. When `tau < dt` the cell's water is fully replaced within one step, which has two consequences that should be applied consistently on both sides.

- Transport side. If `tau < dt`, the cell concentration is inflow-determined, so it should be set to the inflow-weighted donor average regardless of whether that raises or lowers the solver value. This removes the lift-only asymmetry: a flushed cell should track its donors in both directions, not only upward. The lift-only rule is safe only under the unstated assumption that the sole transport artifact is collapse-toward-zero; that assumption should be tested (a spuriously high donor or ghost value can already push a newly-wet cell too high, and lift-only would keep it).
- Kinetics side. If `tau < dt`, skip or scale the surface-flux kinetics, because the cell is flushed before it can equilibrate to surface forcing. This is what Rec B approximates with a fixed `0.05` m depth threshold; the residence-time form is the rigorous version and generalizes correctly to warmer reaches, hotter seasons, and persistent thin stable pools where a fixed depth clamp would wrongly clip real signal.

A single shared `tau < dt` gate, computed once and consumed by both the transport reconstruction and the kinetics skip, would address hot and cold artifacts at the source with one physically-defensible criterion instead of two proxies in two repos. The depth threshold and the lift-only heuristic become special cases of it.

## DOX reaeration integration: forward Euler, not analytical (verified 2026-05-30)

A read-only check settled whether a bad newly-wet DOX value self-heals safely or has its own thin-cell instability. The v3 DOX kernel (`ClearWater-modules/src/clearwater_modules_v3/processes/dox.py`) integrates reaeration by EXPLICIT FORWARD EULER, not an analytical/exponential update:

- `_atm_reaeration_flux = ka_tc * (dox_sat - dox)` (dox.py:506), summed with the other source/sink fluxes into a net `rate` (mg-O2/L/d), then `delta_dox = rate * dt_days` (dox.py:1007) and `dox_new = dox + delta_dox` (dox.py:832). There is no `dox_sat + (dox - dox_sat) * exp(-ka_tc * dt)` form anywhere; the module docstring and the inline comments label it "Forward Euler (pattern C)" (dox.py:702, 830).
- Stabilizer present: `clip_negative_state` (dox.py:837) floors the post-step value at zero with logging. This prevents negative DOX but does NOT prevent the overshoot oscillation above zero that explicit integration of a stiff relaxation produces.
- Consequence: the self-healing argument for DOX holds ONLY when `ka_tc * dt < 2` (the forward-Euler stability bound for this linear relaxation; `< 1` for monotone, non-oscillatory recovery). With the v3 default substep of 5 minutes (`time_step=timedelta(minutes=5)`, dox.py:309) and a high reaeration coefficient (thin, turbulent, windy cell, `ka_tc` of order 100/d), `ka_tc * dt` is about `100/d * (5/1440) d = 0.35`, comfortably stable. At an hourly DOX substep the same `ka_tc` gives `ka_tc * dt` about 4.2, which oscillates and overshoots. So DOX self-heals safely at the 5-minute substep but NOT if DOX is stepped hourly in a thin high-reaeration cell. The substep, not the depth, is the governing knob.
- Thin-water guard: DOX does NOT re-floor depth itself; the depth-divide sources (benthic algae, SOD) blow up as depth approaches 0, and the kernel explicitly delegates the thin-water guard to the caller, "the coupled runner applies the same depth floor TSM uses, `q_net_depth_skip_threshold`" (dox.py:461-471). This is the coupling caveat from the prior round, now confirmed in code: enabling Rec B's thin-water skip on the runner side suppresses the depth-divide DOX sinks in the thinnest cells, and if the runner also skips reaeration there it removes the very self-healing relied on. Whether the runner's depth skip zeroes reaeration too, or only the depth-divide terms, is the open question for the runner (not the kernel).

Corrected physics note (supersedes an earlier claim in this session). Reaeration does NOT normalize DOX dramatically faster than surface heat flux equilibrates temperature. Both are surface-exchange relaxations `dX/dt = (v_transfer / H)(X_eq - X)`, and the 1/H shallow-water speedup is identical for both and cancels. The time-constant ratio is `tau_T / tau_DOX = K_L * rho * c_p / K_H`, which for typical values (`K_L` about 1 m/d, `K_H` about 20 W/m^2/K, `rho*c_p` = 4.18e6 J/m^3/K) is about 2.4 -- DOX recovers maybe 2-3x faster, not orders of magnitude, and the margin shrinks under high wind. The real reason temperature was the problem child is the SOURCE term and its integration, not equilibration speed: the TSM heat injection `q_net*SA*dt/(V*rho*c_p)` scales as 1/V and re-injects every step (an unbounded forcing, what Rec B targets), whereas reaeration has a built-in restoring force toward dox_sat. The dilution argument also applies equally to both: a spike in a tiny-volume cell carries little extensive heat OR oxygen, so its influence on neighbors is volume-weighted down for both. The earlier "DOX self-heals via surface-area-to-volume" framing was wrong on the physics; the correct reasons DOX is lower-priority are (a) the built-in restoring force toward saturation and (b) the rarity of no-donor cells in routed-flow runs.

## Coupled runner DOX configuration (verified 2026-05-30, read-only)

A read-only audit of the coupled runner (`ClearWater-modules-phase2-ESM-streaming/case_studies/santiam_salem/scripts/08_run_coupled_v3_smoke_canonical.py`) settled the two open questions about how DOX is actually stepped in production, and the answer makes the self-healing argument substep-dependent rather than unconditionally safe.

No depth skip reaches DOX. The runner instantiates `DOX(...)` with only `pressure_mb`, `kah_20_user=20.0`, `hydraulic_reaeration_option=1`, and a time step (runner line ~579); no thin-water or depth-skip parameter is passed. DOX runs unconditionally on every cell in the NSM1 loop (runner lines ~1557-1560). Only Temperature gets a post-step wet-mask clamp (runner lines ~1555-1556); DOX output is merely NaN-masked to dry cells afterward (lines ~1593-1597), which does not touch the integration. So the reaeration restoring force toward saturation is intact (good for self-healing), but the depth-divide DOX sinks (benthic algae and SOD, both `1/depth`) are also unguarded in thin wet cells -- the runner only sets a crude `depth_safe = 1.0 m` fallback divisor at fully-dry cells (lines ~1450-1460), not a graded thin-cell guard. Those depth-divide sinks, not reaeration, are the real thin-cell DOX hazard, and they are the DOX analog of the temperature `1/V` problem with no Rec-B-style guard.

DOX is stepped at 1 hour, not the 5-minute v3 default. The runner passes `time_step=nsm1_step`, and `nsm1_step` inherits the master step (1.0 h) with `nsm1_inner=1` (no subcycling) by default (runner lines ~1115-1145, ~1557-1560). The v3 DOX constructor default of 5 minutes is therefore overridden to 3600 s -- 12x coarser.

Stability consequence. The forward-Euler bound is `ka_tc * dt < 2` (smooth, non-oscillatory recovery needs `< 1`). At the 1-hour step actually used: with the configured `kah_20_user = 20/day`, `ka_tc * dt = 20*(1/24) ~ 0.83` -- stable for the baseline. But thin, turbulent, or windy cells raise `ka_tc`; at `ka_tc ~ 48/day` the product reaches 2.0 and DOX overshoots/oscillates in exactly the thin newly-wet cells of interest. The comfortable margin quoted earlier in this document (`ka_tc * dt ~ 0.35`) assumed the 5-minute default; at the production 1-hour substep the margin is about 2.4x smaller and a plausible reaeration coefficient breaches it. Because `kah_20_user` is a flat user value, wind/hydraulic enhancement in shallow cells is the variable that can cross the bound. The reaeration self-healing argument holds at a sufficiently fine substep; at the production 1-hour substep it is marginal, not guaranteed.

Recommended interim runner-config change (one line, no source edit): run NSM1 sub-cycled, `--nsm1-step-hours 0.25` (or finer), which drops `ka_tc * dt` below ~0.5 for any plausible `ka_tc` and removes the oscillation risk at modest cost; equivalently, let DOX use its native 5-minute step. This is a runner argument, not a code change, and preserves the master/transport cadence.

## DOX gap: confirmed theoretical for this case study (2026-05-30)

The "newly-wet DOX should initialize to saturation, not zero" concern was checked on both the data and code sides and is theoretical for the Willamette/Santiam Sept-2008 run.

Data side. The run uses no precipitation-on-grid. The meteorology fed to the model is air temperature, relative humidity, wind, solar, and cloud only; there is no precipitation channel. Direct inspection of the transcoded hydraulics HDF (`santiam_salem_subset_2008-09_hourly.p01.hdf`) found 38 datasets and zero precipitation, rain, or source-like variables. The hydraulics are routed USGS Sept-2008 unsteady flow. The reference validation run additionally ran with `reconstruct_newly_wet = false` (validation plan run-args, line 86) and still produced a near-zero Salem DOX bias (-0.0074), because the Salem validation cell (86521) is a deep channel cell about 218 m from the gauge, not a floodplain-edge cell where the artifacts live.

Code side. ClearWater-riverine cannot originate wetting. Cell volume is read directly from the RAS HDF `Cell Volume` dataset (io/hdf.py:855, 1273-1289) or synthesized from RAS water-surface elevation plus RAS elevation-volume tables (io/hdf.py:1158-1197), never from internal continuity. Point sources are mass-only and explicitly not propagated through the hydrodynamic mesh (constituents.py:556-567; RHS term `Flow_Rate * Concentration` at linalg.py:714-746), so they cannot wet a dry cell. Internal BC is likewise mass-only (commit e981367; model.py:1029-1033, routed through the same point-source RHS path; "volume bookkeeping stays with RAS"). There is no precipitation handling anywhere in the package.

Consequence for this case study. The Santiam-Salem model is routed-flow only, so a cell becomes wet only when RAS reports volume at t+1 from water routed across a face, which leaves an inflow-face donor (or a ghost/BC value) for the gather cascade. The only residual no-donor sub-case here is a block of cells that all wet in the same timestep with no wet-at-t neighbor (an isolated leading-edge patch); the two-pass relaxation already covers fronts that advance one neighbor ring per step, so this is bounded and transient and sits at floodplain edges away from validation stations. The DOX-saturation gap is therefore theoretical for the Santiam-Salem validation specifically.

Scope correction: the conclusion above does NOT generalize. Two distinct precipitation pathways must be separated.

HEC-HMS to HEC-RAS does not create the no-donor case. HMS turns rainfall into flow hydrographs, but those enter RAS as boundary, lateral, or internal inflows, i.e. water that arrives in the 2D domain across faces. The rainfall origin upstream of RAS is invisible to CWR; what matters is that the water crosses a face, which leaves a donor. HMS-fed models still wet cells with donors present.

RAS rain-on-grid (2D direct precipitation) is the real no-donor pathway, and it is a standard, increasingly-used RAS feature (pluvial and urban flooding, direct-rainfall 2D models). Rain lands directly on a dry cell, which accumulates volume with no inflow face and no wet neighbor. CWR reads the resulting RAS volume, has no precipitation input of its own, finds no donor, and DOX and temperature fall back to solver ~0. For rain-on-grid models the gap is REAL, and it must be addressed before CWR is applied to such a model.

Design implications for the rain-on-grid case:

- Detection hook already exists. Because CWR reads volume directly and cannot see why a cell wetted, the only available signal that a cell was rain-wetted rather than flow-wetted is exactly "newly-wet with no inflow-face donor and no wet neighbor", which is the `candidate is None` branch in `reconstruct_newly_wet` (transport.py:199-205). That branch is where a rain-source default belongs.
- The proper fix reads RAS's precipitation field, not just a fallback constant. A rain-on-grid RAS HDF carries a precipitation dataset; CWR should read it (wire it into the HDF path dictionary near io/hdf.py:855) to both identify rain-wetted cells and perform a real volume and mass balance, rather than inferring rain from the no-donor heuristic alone.
- Rainwater is constituent-specific. A rain-wetted cell should initialize to rainwater values, which differ by constituent: DOX approximately saturation via `dox_sat_apha(T, P)` (modules dox.py:172-218), temperature approximately air or wet-bulb temperature (not channel temperature), N and P approximately 0 (harmless), alkalinity low. This is the "source water" generalization: donor concentration for flow-wetting, rainwater concentration for rain-wetting, and a volume-weighted blend when both contribute in the same step.
- Internal-BC inflow is a second no-donor pathway even without rain. Internal BC is mass-only in CWR (volume stays with RAS) and `_gather_signed` only inspects `FLOW_ACROSS_FACE`, so a cell wetted by a RAS internal inflow line gains volume but may leave no face donor, hitting the same `candidate is None` branch. Its correct source water is the internal-BC inflow concentration, which CWR already knows.

## How to tell why a cell wetted (flow vs rain vs internal BC)

The question "did this cell wet from flow or from rainfall" needs reframing first. Rainfall that reaches the 2D domain as HEC-HMS hydrographs (or as RAS boundary, lateral, or uniform-lateral inflow) enters across faces and is indistinguishable from, and equivalent to, any other routed flow: it leaves a face donor and needs no special handling. The only distinction that matters to CWR is how the water physically entered the cell: across a face (donor concentration known) versus as a vertical or internal source applied to the cell (rain-on-grid precipitation, infiltration loss, or an internal-BC injection, whose source concentration is rainwater or the BC value, not a neighbor). So the discriminator is face-arrival vs source-arrival, not "flow vs rainfall."

Three ways to make that determination, in recommended order.

Method 1, per-cell continuity residual (preferred; uses only what CWR already reads). RAS closes a volume balance on each cell: `V(t+1) - V(t) = dt * (sum of signed face flows + precip - infiltration + internal/point BC) + epsilon_RAS`, where `epsilon_RAS` is RAS's own volume-accounting error. CWR already ingests cell volume (`Cell Volume`) and face flows (`FLOW_ACROSS_FACE`), so it can compute per cell per step `net_face = sum of signed face flows` and `dV = (V(t+1) - V(t))/dt`, then `residual = dV - net_face`. If `residual` is within the RAS volume-error tolerance, the cell's water is fully explained by faces (flow-wetted, donor exists). If `residual` is positive and larger than tolerance, that excess is a non-face source (rain or internal BC) and its magnitude is the source volume to use in a mass balance. For a newly-wet cell, `net_face * dt approximately V(t+1)` means flow-wetted; `net_face approximately 0` with `V(t+1) > 0` means source-wetted. This is version-independent, needs no new RAS dataset, and is exactly the quantitative form of the existing `candidate is None` heuristic in `reconstruct_newly_wet`. Caveat: it needs a tolerance band tied to RAS's reported volume-accounting error, and it attributes rain and internal-BC volume to the same residual (use Method 3 to separate them).

Method 2, read RAS's precipitation and infiltration fields directly (authoritative when rain-on-grid is enabled). A rain-on-grid plan HDF carries the meteorology forcing and, in recent RAS 6.x versions, per-cell precipitation and infiltration result time series. This gives the exact rain volume and rate per cell (better than the residual, and it also yields rain temperature and lets infiltration be handled), but it is present only when the model enabled precipitation, and the exact dataset paths vary by RAS version. ACTION REQUIRED: confirm the dataset names against an actual rain-on-grid RAS HDF before relying on this. Likely locations to verify: input meteorology under an `Event Conditions/Meteorology/Precipitation` group in the plan HDF; per-cell results under the same `Results/Unsteady/Output/Output Blocks/Base Output/Unsteady Time Series/2D Flow Areas/<area>/` group that already holds `Cell Volume` and `Face Flow` (candidate names such as a cumulative precipitation depth and a cumulative infiltration depth per cell, unconfirmed). The `rashdf` library (fema-ffrd) and a direct `h5py` dump are the tools to enumerate the exact names. The HDF path dictionary to extend lives near io/hdf.py:855.

Method 3, internal-BC cell set (already known to CWR). The cells fed by each RAS internal-BC line are already bridged into CWR (`internal_bc_cells`, `internal_bc_cells_by_line`, model.py:1204-1244). A no-donor newly-wet cell that is in that set was wetted by the internal BC, and its source concentration is the BC's known inflow concentration. Subtract these from the Method-1 residual so the remainder is attributable to rain.

Critical caveat grounded in the actual files (verified 2026-05-30). RAS 2D output is configurable, and the cell-volume and face-flow datasets that Method 1 needs are OPTIONAL outputs that a modeler may not enable. The raw Corvallis_Santiam plan HDF (`data/hecras_model/Corvallis_Santiam/Corvallis_Santiam.p01.hdf`, area "Perimeter 1") was written with a minimal output set: its 2D results contain only Water Surface, Depth, Face Velocity, Face Shear Stress, and Time Step. It has no `Cell Volume`, no `Face Flow`, no `Cell Volume Error`, and no precipitation or meteorology group at all. CWR therefore already synthesizes volume from water-surface elevation plus the RAS elevation-volume tables (io/hdf.py:1158-1197) and derives face flow from face velocity. Consequences: (1) a continuity residual built on these derived quantities carries the derivation error and needs a generous tolerance, so Method 1 is weaker than it looks unless the RAS run actually wrote `Cell Volume` and `Face Flow`; (2) Method 2 only works when the modeler enabled precipitation and its output; the gridded precip forcing lives under `Event Conditions/Meteorology/Precipitation` (Values, Timestamp, spatial metadata), but a per-cell precipitation or infiltration RESULTS dataset name could not be confirmed from public HEC documentation and must be discovered by dumping an actual rain-on-grid HDF with rashdf or h5py. The robust posture is therefore: when CWR is to be applied to a rain-on-grid model, require the RAS run to output `Cell Volume`, `Face Flow`, and precipitation/infiltration; then Methods 1 and 2 are both available and cross-check each other. Absent those, fall back to the existing no-donor heuristic and document the reduced confidence.

Bottom line: CWR cannot read RAS's intent, but it does not need to. Method 1 distinguishes face-wetting from source-wetting using data already in hand; Method 3 peels off internal-BC cells; Method 2 (after path confirmation) supplies the exact rain volume and rate when precipitation is enabled. Combine them: classify a newly-wet cell as flow-wetted when the face flows explain the volume, internal-BC-wetted when it is in the BC set, and rain-wetted otherwise, then initialize its constituents from the matching source water.
